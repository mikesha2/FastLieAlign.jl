User guide

Data layout

The vectors of frame A are the columns of an $n \times m$ matrix X, those of frame B the columns of Y, with the $i$-th column of each corresponding. X must have full row rank, which needs $m \ge n$ vectors in general position. For the Euclidean and similarity groups, X and Y hold homogeneous coordinates, with a last row of ones.

Each group knows its matrix size, matsize(G), its Lie algebra projection, algebra_projection(G, L), an orthonormal basis of the algebra, algebra_basis(G), and membership tests, in_group(G, g) and in_algebra(G, A). random_group(G; scale) and random_algebra(G) draw test elements.

Alignment methods

FunctionPaperWhat it does
naive_alignAlgorithm 1projected principal logarithm of $Y X^+$; throws when $Y X^+$ has no real logarithm
branch_cut_alignAlgorithm 2factors out the involution $r$ first; exact on the whole group
negative_involution, even_reflectionAlgorithm 3the involution $r$ from the Schur form of $Y X^+$
spin_alignAlgorithm 4the $\mathrm{Spin}(n)$ and $\mathrm{Pin}(n)$ case, through the covering map
gauss_newton_refineAlgorithm 5Gauss–Newton on the group from the Lie algebra estimate
align_newton, align_newton_full, align_direct§4 baselinesNewton with a finite difference or exact Hessian, and BFGS, through the exponential map
align_componentsthe naive method on every component, with the data choosing the best
align_se, align_se_centroid§5.2rigid registration in homogeneous coordinates, and the centroid route

branch_cut_align is the recommended estimator. It reduces to naive_align when $Y X^+$ has no eigenvalue with negative real part, and otherwise it negates those eigenvalues with an involution of the group before taking the logarithm, so that elements of the other connected components, rotations by $\pi$, and elements of $SU(n)$ and $SL(n,\mathbb C)$ whose principal logarithm is not traceless are all recovered exactly from noiseless data.

Accuracy

The Lie algebra estimate has a first order error in the noise (Theorem 3.1 of the paper). For the compact groups $SO(n)$, $U(n)$ and $SU(n)$ with isotropic inputs, $X X^\dagger = c I$, it agrees with the least squares solution over the group to first order (Corollary 3.3). Otherwise gauss_newton_refine reaches that solution, at a cost of a few times the closed form. The refinement is not recommended when the inputs X are themselves noisy, since the least squares objective is then biased.

align_error(g_est, g_true) returns the Frobenius and maximum entry errors.

Complex groups

Complex matrices are handled directly; the projections use the real inner product $\langle A, B\rangle = \mathrm{Re}\,\mathrm{tr}(A^\dagger B)$, because the Lie algebras of the complex groups are real vector spaces.
